What you build is what flies

You place every deck, gun, shield, engine and reactor, and you run the cables that feed them. There are no hidden numbers. A ship is as fast as her engines can push her weight, and as tough as what you put in the way.

Put the reactor in the nose, and the reactor is what gets shot off the nose.

A crew, not a health bar

Stations are manned. Your people carry power and ammunition to the guns, fight the fires and mend the hull. Air leaves through the holes. They eat what your ships grow or buy, and a ship that runs out goes hungry.

Marines board and fight deck by deck, yours and theirs.

A sector that does not wait

The Rust Compact and the Lumen Synod are at war. The Free Rakers raid the frontier. Stations trade, grow and fall, and fleets fight whether you are there to see it or not.

Trade between ports, take contracts and bounties, mine the belts, research new parts, found a colony, or raid a station and live with who hates you for it.

Nothing ends your game for you

There is no timer and no forced run. Lose the whole fleet and a salvage tug picks up your pods and tows you to the nearest friendly port in a battered loaner, for a fifth of your credits. Then you go back out.

Warp gates lead on to the next sector, and each one you left goes on living while you are away.

One fight, many ships

Fly one, command the rest

Take the helm of one ship and give orders to the others. Shields have a facing. A hurt ship is missing the parts that were hit, and stays that way until someone mends her.

The drydock

Build a ship from nothing or refit the one you have. Save the design, and try it in a skirmish before you trust it with a crew.

Skirmish

The demo and the full game

The demo is the whole game: every part and every system, for as long as you like. What it holds back is how far the galaxy goes. A demo galaxy is the sectors it began with, up to nine, and every one of them stays at level 3, so in time your fleet outgrows it.

In the full game each new sector rises to meet the fleet that found it, and there is always another sector past the last gate. Your demo campaign carries straight on.

Questions

Does it cost anything?

The demo is free and has no time limit. The full game is $20, once, bought here.

What does it run on?

A computer with a recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. Nothing to install. It is made for a keyboard and a mouse. Adding the full game needs Chrome or Edge 137, Firefox 130, Safari 17, or newer: an older browser still plays the demo, and says so if you try to add the file.

Your campaigns stay in your browser, and any save can go out to a file and come back. Clearing the browser's data clears them too, so save one out to a file now and then.

Does the game collect anything?

No. The game has no account, no trackers and no ads, and it sends nothing back to us. Flying with friends finds them through public peer to peer servers (WebTorrent trackers and STUN servers), which see your address as any server does, and the game says so in the room's window. With the full game added, the game fetches one update file from this site, the same file for everyone, and sends nothing about you.

Is it made with AI?

Yes, all of it. Every line of the game's code was written by AI (Claude, the lead developer in our small studio's AI crew), directed by a human designer. There isn't a single picture or sound file in the game: the ships, the people, the sky, the music and every sound are made by code as you play.
