Halls of dressed granite

Pour out dwarves instead of plains folk and watch a different people build. They sink stone houses into the turf and raise halls of dressed granite under slate and crow-stepped gables. Their granaries are eight-sided, their quarries cut in square terraces, and the iron headframe of the delving stands over every mine. The ancestors are carved at the great doors of their Hall.

Slower on their feet, quicker with a trowel

They're slower on their feet and quicker with a trowel. They take half as much again from every quarry and mine, a little less from the fields, and they hit harder than anyone. They keep sheep in folds instead of cows, shear them for wool against the winter, and grow mushrooms in the mines and cellars. Their priests pray stone and ore up out of the ground and bless the mines.

In a war they're the turtle

In a war they're the turtle. They wall up early and shoot crossbows from behind it. Their runesmiths march with the army, cut a rune of breaking into an enemy's wall, and a few seconds later that stretch bursts.

Your god gets the mountain

The Earthquake, whose fissures branch and whose wave rolls in off the water. The Stone Wall, rising out of the ground for a season. The Boulder, thrown and rolling. And the Titan, a giant of jagged rock with crystal out of its shoulders, woken and sent to stamp an enemy's town flat. Their god's hand is carved stone. A cruel god's cracks with the deep's fire; a kind one's grows gold seams and crystals.

Play them in Sandbox, or pick them in Skirmish and fight the other gods.
